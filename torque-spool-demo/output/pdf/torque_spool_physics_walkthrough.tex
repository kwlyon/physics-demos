\section*{1. The surprising observation}

A cord is wound around an inner cylinder of radius $r$ while the spool rolls on outer tires of radius $R$. Pull the cord at an angle $\theta$ above the horizontal. Depending on $\theta$, the spool can roll either toward or away from the pull. At one particular angle, called the \emph{critical angle} $\theta_{\mathrm c}$, the direction reverses.

The cleanest way to predict that transition is to calculate torque about the point $P$ where the tire touches the floor. This choice removes the unknown normal force and static-friction force from the torque equation because both act at $P$.

\section*{2. Deriving the critical angle}

At the transition, extend the cord's line of action backward. It passes exactly through $P$ and remains tangent to the winding cylinder at $A$. The radius $OA$ is perpendicular to the tangent line, so triangle $OAP$ is right-angled at $A$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.15,>=Latex,font=\small]
  \coordinate (O) at (0,0);
  \coordinate (P) at (0,-2.8);
  \coordinate (A) at (1.4,-1.4);

  \fill[softblue] (O) circle (2.8);
  \draw[navy,very thick] (O) circle (2.8);
  \fill[white] (O) circle (1.98);
  \draw[blueaccent,very thick] (O) circle (1.98);
  \draw[darkgray,thick] (-3.35,-2.8)--(4.0,-2.8);
  \draw[orangeaccent,very thick,-{Latex[length=3mm]}] (P)--(4.2,1.4)
    node[pos=0.86,above left] {$\vec T$};
  \draw[darkgray,dashed,thick] (O)--(A);
  \draw[darkgray,dashed,thick] (O)--(P);
  \fill[navy] (O) circle (1.7pt) node[above left] {$O$};
  \fill[orangeaccent] (P) circle (2pt) node[below] {$P$};
  \fill[blueaccent] (A) circle (1.7pt) node[above left] {$A$};

  \node[left] at (-0.05,-1.4) {$R$};
  \node[above right] at (0.67,-0.63) {$r$};
  \draw[darkgray] ($(P)+(0.72,0)$) arc[start angle=0,end angle=45,radius=0.72];
  \node at ($(P)+(0.98,0.38)$) {$\theta_{\mathrm c}$};
  \draw[darkgray,thick] ($(A)+(-0.14,0.14)$)--($(A)+(0,0.28)$)--($(A)+(0.14,0.14)$);

  \node[align=left,text width=5.2cm,anchor=west] at (4.65,0.25) {
    In right triangle $OAP$:
    \[
      OP=R,\qquad OA=r.
    \]
    Since $\angle OPA=90^\circ-\theta_{\mathrm c}$,
    \[
      \frac{r}{R}
      =\sin(90^\circ-\theta_{\mathrm c})
      =\cos\theta_{\mathrm c}.
    \]
  };
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

Solving for the critical angle gives the design equation

\keybox{\LARGE $\displaystyle
\theta_{\mathrm c}
=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)
=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{2r}{D}\right).$}

The same result follows from the perpendicular moment arm of the tension. If $d_T$ is the signed distance from $P$ to the cord's line of action, then
\[
  \tau_P=T d_T.
\]
As the pull angle crosses $\theta_{\mathrm c}$, $d_T$ changes sign. The torque from the pull therefore changes sign, producing the observed reversal.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Pull angle & Line of action relative to $P$ & Tendency \\
\midrule
$\theta<\theta_{\mathrm c}$ & one side of $P$ & rotation in one sense \\
$\theta=\theta_{\mathrm c}$ & through $P$ & zero torque from tension \\
$\theta>\theta_{\mathrm c}$ & opposite side of $P$ & reversed rotation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section*{3. Numerical check for the 45-degree insert}

The measured outside diameter of the installed TPU tires and the corresponding rolling radius are
\[
  D=185.0\,\mathrm{mm},
  \qquad
  R=\frac{D}{2}=92.5\,\mathrm{mm}.
\]
The 45-degree insert has a designed winding-surface radius
\[
  r=65.407\,\mathrm{mm}
  \qquad
  \left(2r=130.815\,\mathrm{mm}\right).
\]
Therefore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \theta_{\mathrm c}
  &=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)\\[2pt]
  &=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{65.407\,\mathrm{mm}}{92.5\,\mathrm{mm}}\right)\\[2pt]
  &=45.00^\circ.
\end{aligned}
\]

\keybox{The modeled 45-degree insert reproduces a $45.00^\circ$ critical angle when $r$ is taken at the bare winding surface.}

\section*{4. What changes in a real demonstration?}

The cord does not act at the bare plastic surface. For cord diameter $d$, the first-layer cord centerline is approximately
\[
  r_{\mathrm{eff}}=r+\frac{d}{2}.
\]
For example, a $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ cord on the 45-degree insert gives
\[
  r_{\mathrm{eff}}=66.407\,\mathrm{mm},
  \qquad
  \theta_{\mathrm c,eff}
  =\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{66.407}{92.5}\right)
  \approx44.12^\circ.
\]
Additional winding layers increase $r_{\mathrm{eff}}$ further and reduce the predicted critical angle.

\section*{5. Suggested classroom procedure}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.55em,itemsep=3pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item Install the 45-degree insert and both TPU tires. Use a thin cord in a single neat layer.
  \item Pull slowly at an angle clearly below $45^\circ$ and note the rolling direction.
  \item Repeat clearly above $45^\circ$ and observe the reversal.
  \item Approach the transition in small angular steps. Compare the measured angle with $45.00^\circ$, or with the cord-corrected prediction if the cord diameter is known.
  \item Discuss why choosing the floor-contact point for the torque calculation eliminates the normal and friction forces from the rotational analysis.
\end{enumerate}
